Six from Westwood

UCLA put six players into the 2026 WNBA draft, more than any school ever has. They arrived as a cohort and get discussed as one. Ten weeks of possession data says they had six different seasons — and that the honest version of this story is partly about which differences are real.

One

They were drafted together. Nothing after that was shared.

The six landed on four teams, and the rotations sorted them immediately. Kiki Rice played 26.3 minutes a game in Toronto. Gianna Kneepkens played 8.3 in Connecticut. In between, three of the six saw their minutes fall through the season and two saw them rise — and the shapes are so different that any sentence beginning "the UCLA rookies" is already wrong.

Read the panels below as trajectories rather than levels. Lauren Betts and Charlisse Leger-Walker were trusted with more as the season went on. Gabriela Jaquez and Angela Dugalic were trusted with steadily less — Jaquez from 28.3 minutes in May to 11.8 in August, Dugalic all the way down to 3.3. Rice's gap is an injury, and gets its own section.

Two

The looks you get, and what you do with them

Two things get folded together when people talk about efficiency: the quality of the shots a player takes, and how well she converts relative to that quality. In this league they are close to independent — across the 149-player reference pool they correlate at just 0.15. So they are worth plotting against each other, and the quadrants mean something.

Betts sits in the hardest one. Her average shot is worth 0.483 expected points — 15th percentile, among the worst diets in the league — and she converts +0.068 above that expectation, which is 85th percentile. She is taking bad looks and making them anyway. Jaquez is the mirror image: 80th-percentile shot quality, 14th-percentile conversion.

Three

Betts is two different players at the two ends

Split her regularised plus-minus into its offensive and defensive halves and the cohort's widest gap opens up: +1.95 on defence, −2.46 on offence. The defensive number is the best of the six by a distance. The offensive number is near the worst.

The spacing cost is not a subtlety here. Betts attempted zero three-pointers in 622 minutes — one of only five players in the 149-player reference pool who did not take a single one. A centre who cannot be guarded away from the rim shrinks the floor for everyone else, and that shows up in the offensive coefficient no matter how well she finishes at it.

The development arc underneath it is the most encouraging thing in the cohort. Across four months Betts's true shooting climbed 0.52 → 0.59 → 0.61 → 0.64 while her foul rate fell 4.24 → 4.01 → 3.15 → 1.42 per 75 possessions. Scoring efficiency up and fouling down, at the same time, is the pairing that usually means a rookie big has stopped guessing.

Four

Rice's collapse was a minutes restriction

Toronto lost Rice for sixteen games. When she came back, her per-possession numbers looked alarming — true shooting down from 0.67 to 0.46, rim accuracy from 71% to 56%, turnovers more than doubled. Read as a season arc, that is a player who came back broken.

Read against her minutes, it is something much more ordinary. Her first five games back were a capped ramp: 17, 23, 27, 23, 18 minutes. From the sixth game on she was at 29.6 minutes a game, and the rates came back with the workload — true shooting to 0.61, rim accuracy to 94%, turnovers to 1.05 per 75. Rice finishes as the only one of the six with a positive wins-above-average figure, and the only one whose team was clearly better with her on the floor. On a 11-win Toronto team, that is worth more than it looks.

Five

One pairing is a real problem. The rest is noise.

Washington drafted two of the six and plays them together. Over 224 possessions with both Betts and Dugalic on the floor, the Mystics are −20.0 per 100. With Betts on and Dugalic off, they are +2.6. The damage is almost entirely defensive: 114.7 allowed per 100, against 107.4 for the team's other minutes.

That gap is large enough to survive the noise floor, and it has held its sign and rough magnitude across four separate data vintages and a correctness fix to how possessions are attributed. It is the one pairing finding in this cohort worth printing.

The others are not. Every remaining duo gap sits under about 15 points per 100, which on these sample sizes is indistinguishable from nothing. A "two-big" storyline about Betts and Shakira Austin looked compelling on partial-season data and evaporated on the full season — across four vintages that estimate read +1.6, −1.6, −0.1 and +4.7. It changed sign twice.

Seven

How much of this should you believe?

Every claim above carries a chip, and the chips are not decoration. This pipeline rebuilds on new data most days, and rebuilding the same analysis on successive vintages turned out to be the most useful robustness check available. Some numbers reproduce exactly. Others move by several points on four extra games. Knowing which is which is most of the analytical work.

What reproduces, and what doesn't

Stable: minutes, shooting splits, shot diet, foul rates, and the one well-sampled pairing. These reproduced across every vintage tested.

Unstable: anything resting on a few hundred possessions. Rice's season on/off swing read +11.4, then +7.8, then +5.7 across three vintages. Same player, same method, four extra games each time.

Not resolvable: the ordering of these six against each other on impact metrics. Six players inside a 1.5-point band, at a split-half reliability of , is not a ranking. It is a band.

The impact layer was checked against an independently published 2026 RAPM covering the league's top players. Of players present in both, fall inside that reference's own published uncertainty interval, at essentially zero bias. Worth noting what the reference's own error bars say, though: it publishes a standard error of , and its entire top-24 spans standard errors. By its own arithmetic that group is not rank-orderable either — which is the same caution, arriving from outside.

One honest gap: on the four players in that comparison with fewer than twenty games, this model runs about 1.5 points lower than the reference. Ridge shrinkage toward zero is the obvious explanation and it does not survive checking — these estimates only shrink materially below about 300 possessions, and three of the four sit well above that. The cause is open. Treat low-sample players here as the least trustworthy rows in the set.

Method. Built on the pbpstats game layer for games through . Lineup and pairing figures come from a possession layer reconstructed from play-by-play — possessions, both five-player lineups carried on every row, calibrated so team totals match the pbpstats scale exactly. Impact figures are ridge-regularised plus-minus fitted in-repo on that layer, cross-validated by game rather than by possession, and calibrated separately on each side of the ball.

Season aggregates are possession-weighted throughout, never averages of per-game rates. Percentiles are against the -player league pool (250+ minutes); higher always means better. Box-score plus-minus is deliberately absent — it cross-validates at R² below zero on a single season, which means it would be a decorated guess.
